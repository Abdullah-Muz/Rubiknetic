## 1. NoSlip in practice (MuJoCo)

### 1.1 What NoSlip does in 4.0.1 (official docs and source)

- FACT. Computation chapter, 4.0.1 (https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/4.0.1/doc/computation/index.rst, section `soNoSlip`): "After the main solver (Newton, CG, or PGS) has converged, the NoSlip solver re-solves the friction dimensions only, using a PGS sweep with R = 0 (i.e., hard constraints) in those dimensions. ... this cascade of optimization steps no longer solves a single well-defined optimization problem; it is an ad-hoc correction that can occasionally cause instabilities in models with complex multi-contact interactions." Establishes: NoSlip is a post-pass and has no guarantee of correctness or stability.
- FACT. Source, 4.0.1 (https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/4.0.1/src/engine/engine_solver.c, `solNoSlip`, around lines 778-975): one sweep updates (a) **dry-friction rows (joint/tendon `frictionloss`)**, clamped to +/-frictionloss, then (b) **contact friction rows**; for elliptic cones it solves a per-contact QCQP over the `dim-1` tangential (and torsional/rolling) components with the normal force held fixed; equality rows and contact normal rows are not updated. Sweeps go in fixed sequential order (`for c = ne ... nefc`). It stops at `noslip_iterations` or when the scaled improvement drops below `noslip_tolerance`. Islands are supported (`mj_solNoSlip_island`). Establishes: NoSlip also makes joint `frictionloss` hard, not just contact friction, and it works with elliptic cones and Newton.
- FACT. Modeling chapter, 4.0.1 (local `modeling_401.rst`, lines 1748-1755, section "Preventing slip"): "A small number (1, 2 or 3) is usually sufficient. ... the iteration limit caps the work, while noslip_tolerance is an early-termination threshold rather than a bound on residual slip. Exact zero slip is not guaranteed. NoSlip also adds computational cost, makes inverse dynamics ill-defined and can occasionally cause instabilities in complex multi-contact systems." Also lines 1979-1986: "`noslip_iterations` of `1-3` suppresses drift strongly, at roughly 2x step time, ill-defined inverse dynamics, and occasional chatter in overconstrained grasps." And lines 553-556: the order to try is "elliptic cones, large impratio, and the Newton algorithm with very small tolerance. If that is not sufficient, enable the Noslip solver." Establishes: official recommended range is 1-3; cost about 2x step time; the known failure mode for grasps is "chatter in overconstrained grasps". The project's current setup (elliptic, impratio 10, Newton, tol 1e-10) is exactly the documented first stage.
- FACT. Same file, lines 584-587: NoSlip is a PGS solver, so the PGS setup cost (inverse inertia in constraint space) is paid whenever it is on, even with Newton.

### 1.2 Reported problems (GitHub issues)

- FACT. Issue #2423 "Confusing unphysical interaction of noslip, frictionloss, and damping" (open, opened 2025-02-14, MuJoCo 3.2.7): https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/2423. A single free hinge with `frictionloss` > 0, `damping` > 0 and `noslip_iterations` > 0 (integrator `implicit`) decelerates far too slowly; turning off any one of the three removes it. A third-party comment (2026-11-11, https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/2423#issuecomment-6790336005) reports reproducing it on **3.17.0 and 4.0.0**: velocity after 100 steps 0.0961 rad/s with NoSlip vs 2.96e-6 without; "Ten NoSlip iterations give the same slow decay"; it attributes it to NoSlip combined with implicit damping because "implicit integration omits constraint-force derivatives". No maintainer response is posted yet. Establishes: a known, unresolved, reproducible-in-4.0.0 artefact for joints that have both frictionloss and damping under NoSlip with an implicit integrator. (Not tested with `implicitfast`; see INFERENCE below.)
- FACT. Issue #2430 "Setting frictionloss breaks joint equality constraint" (closed, 3.2.7): https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/2430. The reporter found that "In the `noslip` postprocessing step, the `qfrc_constraint` for the DOF with `frictionloss` is set to (near) zero, while the other DOF's `qfrc_constraint` is untouched, which completely breaks the equality constraint" (https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/2430#issuecomment-2665265323). Maintainer Yuval Tassa: "Your diagnosis is correct, this is basically due to how `noslip` is implemented. As far as I know there is no way (even in principle) to merge say the full PGS solve with a noslip step ... So I currently don't see a way to avoid it..." (https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/2430#issuecomment-2676417556). Establishes: NoSlip can visibly violate equality constraints that are coupled to friction-carrying DOFs, by design.
- FACT. Issue #3328 "Regularized friction never sticks ..." (open, opened 2026-06-10, MuJoCo 3.8.1): https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/3328. A pinched capsule at 20-95x the Coulomb static margin creeps at ~50-155 um/s with `cone="elliptic" impratio="10"`, creep "scales approximately linearly with 1/impratio", and "`noslip_iterations="10"` reduces it ~110x (to 1.4 um/s)". Maintainer: "This is WAI [working as intended] but I agree that the documentation is a bit spread out" (https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/3328#issuecomment-4735966682). Establishes: the creep D13 observed is the documented behaviour of regularised friction, not a bug; 10 NoSlip iterations strongly reduce (not eliminate) it in a pinch-grasp. The 4.0.1 "Preventing slip" text quoted above appears to be the resulting doc update (INFERENCE from wording; the PR was not checked).
- FACT. Issue #3901 (closed, 4.0.0): https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/3901. Setup "implicitfast, elliptic cone, impratio=10, noslip_iterations=4" with Newton; a resting contact stack diverged when islands were on. Reporter: "Not noslip: it still diverges with `noslip_iterations=0`". Maintainer: "This is already fixed in 9ae9bad01 (and is live in 4.0.1)". The 4.0.1 changelog entry 3 describes that fix as a sparse-Cholesky bug "introduced in 4.0.0 ... causing slow convergence, wrong accelerations and divergence". Establishes: a 4.0.0 Newton divergence in a configuration close to the project's was a Cholesky bug, not NoSlip, and is fixed in 4.0.1.
- FACT. Issue #698 (closed, 2023): https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/698. Erez (collaborator) on grasp slip: "You should try adding "noslip_iterations=2" (or higher, if necessary): it was designed exactly to remedy this mode of failure." (https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/698#issuecomment-1410003854). Tassa (collaborator): "reduce `noslip_iterations` to 3. More than 3 will just slow you down and have no other effect." (https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/698#issuecomment-1423145326). Establishes: developer guidance (2023, MuJoCo 2.3.x) that values above 3 rarely help. Contrast with #3328 (3.8.1), where 10 iterations were used and reported effective; whether 3 would have done the same there is not reported.
- FACT. Issue #941 (closed, 2023): https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/941. A user measuring gripper contact forces: "I've tried setting `noslip_iterations` to various values > 0, but the results were never consistently better, sometimes they were even worse." The root cause turned out to be finger geometry. Establishes (weakly, user report): NoSlip does not fix noisy grasp forces from other causes.
- FACT. Issue #2934 (open, 3.3.7): https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/2934. With `implicitfast`, Newton, elliptic cone, `noslip_iterations="2"`, an object held by a Robotiq 2F-85 slides ahead of the fingers when the wrist is rotated by position actuators (not with mocap). Unresolved; maintainer asked for an XML-only reproduction (https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/2934#issuecomment-4183566032). A contributor reports similar "cube would move up (defying gravity)" behaviour once. Establishes only that an unexplained grasp-while-rotating artefact has been reported with NoSlip on; NoSlip was not isolated as the cause.
- FACT. Issue #565 (closed, 2022): https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/565. Tassa: noslip "it'll probably be a bit slow, since the noslip solver is based on PGS."
- FACT. Discussions searched (GraphQL, query "noslip"): #3248, #2677, #2309, #3296 are grasp-slip questions; none contains a maintainer-quantified NoSlip result. Discussion #2309 answer only points to the "Preventing slip" docs (https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/discussions/2309).
- UNVERIFIED. I found no issue reporting **energy gain** specifically attributable to NoSlip, and none on NoSlip with **condim 4 (torsional) pads** turning an object. Absence in a keyword search is not evidence that it does not happen.

### 1.3 Changes between 3.x and 4.0.x relevant to NoSlip (changelog at tag 4.0.1)

- FACT. 3.17.0 (Oct 22, 2026), commit c8362756d: "Constraint solvers now scale their termination criteria by the trace of the current inertia matrix (of the model or island) instead of mjModel.stat.meaninertia. Models solved with PGS, noslip or islands disabled can stop at slightly different iterations." Source: https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/4.0.1/doc/changelog.rst (lines ~526-528). Establishes: `noslip_tolerance` early termination changed meaning relative to 3.16 and earlier; the same `noslip_tolerance` can stop at a different iteration count.
- FACT. 3.9.0: `mjData.efc_Y` allocated when "dual solvers (PGS or NoSlip)" are used; added the `diagexact` flag (exact constraint-space diagonal, used for PGS/NoSlip-style diagonal preconditioning). Same changelog, lines ~1618-1622.
- FACT. 3.8.1 and 3.9.0: the main PGS solver iterates in pseudo-random order with a fixed seed. The NoSlip routine in 4.0.1 iterates in fixed sequential order (source above). The modeling doc notes PGS "performs sequential updates, and therefore breaks symmetry" (local modeling_401.rst lines 572-574).
- FACT. 4.0.1 fixed the 4.0.0 sparse-Cholesky bug (see #3901). No changelog entry between 3.0 and 4.0.1 changes the NoSlip algorithm itself (grep of "noslip" in the changelog finds only the two entries above).

### 1.4 Inferences

- INFERENCE. The D13 result (creep removed at noslip 10) is consistent with #3328 and the 4.0.1 docs. Official guidance says 1-3 is usually enough; 10 is outside the recommended range but there is no report that 10 is harmful, only that it costs more (doc: ~2x step time at 1-3).
- INFERENCE. If the cube model uses joint `frictionloss` together with joint `damping` on any DOF (pieces or arm/gripper joints), #2423 is a direct risk: under NoSlip with an implicit-type integrator, free motion of such a DOF may decay far too slowly. #2423 was shown with `implicit`; `implicitfast` also treats damping implicitly and also omits constraint-force derivatives, so it is likely, but not shown, to behave the same way.
- INFERENCE. If constraint activation in the cube model (equality constraints representing connectivity) is coupled to DOFs or contacts that carry friction, #2430 says NoSlip can redistribute friction forces in ways that violate those equalities, because equality rows are not re-solved in the NoSlip pass. That matters for the CLAUDE.md requirement to audit constraint impulses and illegal topology.
- INFERENCE. During a turn the pad contacts (condim 4, mu 0.8) and the plastic-plastic contacts (condim 3, mu 0.2) are all re-solved as hard friction. This removes creep but may change the face's breakaway torque and stick-slip timing compared with noslip 0. "Chatter in overconstrained grasps" (doc) is the failure mode to look for: two pads plus many internal contacts form an overconstrained system.

## 2. Physical friction of speedcube internals

- UNVERIFIED (not found). I found **no primary measurement** (paper or manufacturer datasheet) of ABS-on-ABS or PC-on-PC friction coefficients, static or kinetic, dry or with silicone lubricant, and no manufacturer statement of speedcube plastic grade. Search snippets suggested related work only:
  - UNVERIFIED (search snippet, not opened): a pin-on-disc comparison of 3D-printed filaments reporting ABS ~0.4 (counterface not confirmed, probably steel); Leeds/White Rose "Friction and Wear of Additive Manufactured Polymers in Dry Contact" (https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/176565/3/Friction%20and%20Wear%20of%20Additive%20Manufactured%20Polymers%20in%20Dry%20Contact.pdf), ABS against 52100 steel. Neither is self-mated, moulded or lubricated.
  - UNVERIFIED (search snippet): a thesis reporting PC-on-steel dynamic friction of 0.50-0.55, reduced to 0.35 with slip agents (https://acikbilim.yok.gov.tr/handle/20.500.12812/380950). Not PC-on-PC.
  - UNVERIFIED (search snippet): a patent describing a PC/silicone-oil surface with COF <= 0.06 at 3.0 MPa and 0.02 m/s (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9187634). Patent claim, not an independent measurement, and not cube plastic.
  - UNVERIFIED (search snippet): RTP Company workshop slides (https://rtpcompany.com/wp-content/uploads/RTP-Company_Healthcare-Workshop_Low-Friction-Thermoplastics-for-Single-Use-Devices.pdf) say self-on-self neat resin pairs gave the worst friction and use a <= 0.15 static target for low-friction compounds. No ABS/PC numbers extracted.
  - UNVERIFIED (weak, engineering forum): resin makers are reluctant to list coefficients; "Polycarbonate on polycarbonate is not going to be very good" (https://www.eng-tips.com/threads/plastic-plastic-friction-vs-plastic-steel-friction.286380).
- WEAK (vendor blogs, not measurements): speedcube lubes are mostly silicone-based with viscosity grades; heavier lube slows turning, lighter lube is faster but dries out sooner (e.g. https://speedcubeshop.com/a/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-speed-cube-lubricant, https://cubelelo.com/blogs/cubing/how-to-set-up-speedcubes, https://cuboss.com/shop/luboss-silicone-oil-lubricant/). These establish only that real cube internals run in a viscous (speed-dependent) lubricated regime, not a friction coefficient.
- UNVERIFIED (not found). Nothing measured on whether magnetic speedcube pieces **creep or settle at rest**. One forum anecdote (SpeedSolving) about a MagLev cube feeling sticky after use is irrelevant to rest creep.
- INFERENCE. The simulation's mu = 0.2 between plastic pieces has no primary source behind it from this search. A silicone-lubricated contact is better described as viscous/boundary-lubricated (friction depends on speed, with a static value above the kinetic one) than as a single Coulomb mu. A real magnetic cube at rest is held by magnets and Coulomb-like static friction. There is no known physical mechanism for monotone gravity-driven creep of a few tenths of a degree at a resting face, apart from the small cushion of tolerances that magnets are designed to centre. So D13's creep is plausibly a simulation artefact, in line with D13's own conclusion, but no physical measurement backs this. A small bench measurement (inclined-plane static angle on two cube pieces, dry and lubed) would be the cheapest way to get a primary value.

## 3. Published simulations of Rubik's cube internal mechanics

- FACT. OpenAI et al., "Solving Rubik's Cube with a Robot Hand", arXiv:1910.07113 (2019), https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07113 (read via https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.07113). Section 4.2: "our simulated model consists of 26 rigid body convex cubelets"; "Six central cubelets have a single hinge joint representing a single rotational degree of freedom"; "All remaining 20 corner and edge cubelets have three hinge joints corresponding to full Euler angle representation"; total "6x1+20x3=66 degrees of freedom". "We do not implement any custom physics in our modelling, but rely on the cubelet shapes, contact forces and friction." The cube's internal pressure "results in a certain base level of friction in the system both between the cubelets and in the joints." Spring joints for deformation were tried and "native MuJoCo soft contacts already exhibit similar dynamics". Edges were "beveled ... 1.425 mm inwards". Calibration was "very rudimentary". Establishes: a MuJoCo cube built from contact plus joint friction, with no reported friction coefficients.
- FACT. Same paper, Appendix B / Table 9 (as extracted by the fetch tool; I did not inspect the table directly): randomized parameters include "Dof damping cube", "Dof friction loss cube", "Dof armature cube", "Geom gap cube", "Friction cube"; Appendix B.1.2: "The weights for cube friction is w=1.0 for slide and w=2.0 for spin and roll" in x = x0*exp(w*lambda). Establishes: OpenAI modelled cube DOF friction as joint `frictionloss` plus damping and armature, and randomized it. Nominal values and solver options (noslip, cone, impratio) are **not reported** in the text I read.
- INFERENCE. OpenAI's cube used joint `frictionloss` plus damping on cubelet joints, which is the exact combination in #2423. If Rubiknetic copies that pattern and enables NoSlip, the #2423 artefact applies.
- UNVERIFIED. arXiv:2610.24610 "Solving a Giant Rubik's Cube with a Quadruped Robot" (Amiet et al., submitted 17 Oct 2026; https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24610) trains RL skills in simulation; the abstract does not state the engine, cube joint model or friction settings. Full text not read.
- UNVERIFIED (not found). No other paper found that reports internal cube friction settings in MuJoCo, PyBullet, Isaac or Drake. The search was brief, so this is not exhaustive.

## Implications for Stage 2 (all INFERENCE)

1. The literature backs the D13 finding (#3328, 4.0.1 docs): creep under regularised friction is expected behaviour, and NoSlip is the documented remedy. Stage 2 should still treat NoSlip as an ad-hoc post-pass ("no longer solves a well-defined optimization problem"). Any face-turn result obtained with it should be reported as conditional on that setting, not as better physics.
2. Before Stage 2, check whether any DOF in the trial models (cube pieces, gripper fingers, xArm joints) has **both `frictionloss` and `damping`**. If so, #2423 (reproduced on 4.0.0, unresolved) is a confound. Add a free-decay check of such a DOF at noslip 0 vs 10 as a cheap static gate.
3. If cube connectivity uses equality constraints, add a per-step metric of equality-constraint violation, noslip on vs off (#2430: NoSlip can break equalities coupled to friction-carrying DOFs). This also serves the CLAUDE.md requirement to audit constraint activation.
4. Official guidance is 1-3 iterations; 10 is unusual. Consider a small iteration sweep on a subset (e.g. 2, 3, 10), or at least log the actual NoSlip iteration count and termination improvement (`solver_niter` includes NoSlip iterations in 4.0.1). This shows whether 10 is binding or stops early on `noslip_tolerance`. Note that 3.17.0 changed how the tolerance is scaled.
5. For turning and gripping trials, watch for "chatter in overconstrained grasps": pad contact-force noise, finger joint oscillation, and breakaway torque of the turned face, noslip on vs off. Also watch for the unexplained object-sliding-while-rotating artefact (#2934, NoSlip on, unresolved). Check energy and angular momentum across the turn, since no source rules out energy injection.
6. NoSlip uses a fixed sequential sweep order. Results may depend weakly on constraint/contact ordering. A permutation or symmetry check (e.g. mirrored setups) would expose that.
7. Expect roughly 2x step cost (doc, for 1-3 iterations) and possibly more at 10. The draft's cost estimate should be checked against this.
8. The friction coefficient mu = 0.2 for lubricated plastic currently has no primary source. Record it as an ASSUMPTION, and consider a bench measurement before using it as a validated parameter.
