# Real-cube measurement guide (open item O-09)

**Purpose:** give the simulated cubes (M2, D5) real targets instead of guesses.

**What you need:** the 3×3 cube the robot will use, a luggage scale or kitchen scale (grams), strong thread
or fishing line, sticky tape, a ruler, and a phone with a protractor or level app. It takes about 30–45
minutes.

**For every measurement:** write down the cube model and brand, whether it is new or worn, any lubricant,
and the room temperature. Repeat each measurement 3 times and record all three values.

---

## 1. Turning torque (the most important)

1. Wrap the thread **3 times around the middle of the top layer**, so it sits in the groove-free band of
   the side stickers, and tape the end down.
2. Hold the rest of the cube firmly. Clamp it, or hold the bottom two layers flat on the table.
3. Pull the thread horizontally with the luggage scale, slowly and steadily, so the layer turns.
4. Record:
   * **Breakaway reading:** the peak just before the layer starts moving.
   * **Running reading:** the steady value while it turns.
5. Repeat for one side face.

**Convert:** torque (N·m) = scale reading (kg) × 9.81 × 0.0285 m (the cube's half-width). For example,
0.2 kg comes to about 0.056 N·m. If you used a kitchen scale under the pulled cube instead, note the
method.

## 2. Forgiveness (corner cutting) angle

1. Turn the top layer slightly off square, using the protractor app on the top face (or marks on paper
   underneath).
2. Try to turn the right face with light finger force.
3. Find the **largest misalignment where the right turn still goes through**. Increase in steps of about
   5° to 10°, then narrow it down.
4. Test both directions of misalignment (clockwise and anticlockwise). Real cubes often differ between
   the two ("reverse corner cutting").

## 3. Corner-twist resistance

1. Hold the cube still and grip one corner between thumb and finger.
2. Try to twist it about the cube's diagonal, as if spinning the corner in place. Push firmly but gently;
   stop before anything pops.
3. Record roughly how far it rotates (in degrees, eyeballed) and whether it springs back when released.
4. **Optional, with the scale:** tape the thread around the corner piece and pull tangentially. Record the
   reading at about 5° of twist, or at the pop if it pops.

## 4. Play (looseness)

1. With the cube solved, wiggle one edge piece in and out (radially) with a fingernail.
2. Measure the gap you can see with a ruler or feeler gauge if you have one; otherwise describe it as
   none, slight or obvious.

## 5. Mass and size

1. Weigh the whole cube.
2. Measure the side length with the ruler.
